Anthropic will spend $11.6 billion on Akamai's cloud over seven years

The company behind Claude just locked in a $11.6 billion computing bill, and the supplier is handing back equity as a sweetener. The structure shows who is really financing the AI buildout, and who ends up holding the risk when a lab's compute bill comes due.

Checked Anthropic committed to $11.6 billion over seven years — more than six times the $1.8 billion Bloomberg reported in May — and Akamai issued it a warrant for up to about 5% of its stock at $111.33 a share.

Nish I'm reading this as the clearest signal yet that running frontier AI is now a capital problem, not just a software one. When a cloud supplier hands you a piece of itself to keep you spending, everyone knows the seven-year bill is the real product.

But The commitment is not guaranteed: Akamai's filing ties it to delivery targets, either side can exit under conditions, and it brings Akamai no revenue this year.

The same AI task can cost 30 times more depending on which agent runs it

McKinsey says companies that hand jobs to AI agents are seeing wildly different bills for the same work, and that AI spending has become a line item executives now track. Their survey found a third of organizations spend more than 10% of their technology budget on AI.

Checked McKinsey found the cost of completing the same task with different agents can differ by up to 30x; a third of organizations spend more than 10% of their tech budget on AI, and 60% plan to increase that spend next year.

Nish This is the number I keep coming back to when I price anything built on AI. If the same outcome can cost a first-time buyer 30x what it costs me, my margin is a guess until I measure my own runs, not the vendor's pitch.

But This is a McKinsey survey reported by Business Insider — the consultancy sells AI advice, so read the framing accordingly.

Google is testing checkout from Flipkart inside Gemini in India

Shoppers in India can now see a Buy button on some Flipkart listings inside Google's AI assistant, tapping through to Flipkart's own checkout without leaving the chat. Google says a wider rollout is planned for later in October, ahead of the festive shopping season.

Checked TechCrunch saw a Buy button on select Flipkart listings for a few Indian users — smartphones, electronics and mobile accessories — with a broader rollout planned for October; Google invested about $350 million in Flipkart in 2024.

Nish I keep an eye on India because that is where checkout friction actually decides who wins. If buying inside the assistant becomes normal here first, any product of mine that only sends people to a webpage is starting from behind.

But It is a limited test — only a few users and product types see the button — and Google would not say what technology powers it or confirm a timeline beyond a comment.

Meta's Muse AI app passed 3 million downloads weeks after launch

Meta is pushing its new AI assistant hard inside Facebook and Instagram, and it has climbed to the top of both the US App Store and Google Play. Sensor Tower saw daily active users jump 27% the day after Meta's Connect conference, though the download estimates vary by tracker.

Checked Sensor Tower counted more than 3.4 million Muse downloads since its September 8 launch and saw daily active users rise 27% the day after Meta Connect; the app has held the top U.S. App Store spot since September 18.

Nish I read Muse as a lesson in distribution over invention — the app is not obviously smarter than ChatGPT, it is simply handed to hundreds of millions of users for free. My products will never have that, so my launch has to do the work their ad network does.

But The app is only in the U.S. and Canada, Meta is boosting it with its own social apps, and the three tracking firms put downloads anywhere from 2.3 to 4.3 million.

ToolsFireworks AI

Fireworks says Ember-1 matches Kimi K3 quality with 40% fewer tokens

Fireworks trained a specialized version of Kimi K3 that reasons more briefly, claiming the same answers for far less compute. Tokens — the chunks of text a model charges for — decide whether a long-running agent pays for itself, so cutting them by 40% is a margin change, not a footnote.

Checked Fireworks says Ember-1 delivers Kimi K3's quality with 40% fewer tokens, and that across seven benchmarks and two customers' production traffic, K3's reasoning could be shortened 35–50% without losing accuracy.

Nish Here is the rare tools story that changes what I do on Monday: if I get the same answer for fewer tokens, I can let an agent run longer loops for the same bill. I will re-benchmark my own tasks first, but the direction is what I want.

But The numbers are Fireworks' own, and commenters on Hacker News rightly asked what capability is traded away for the shorter reasoning — shorter thinking is not free.

Google's AI answered a basketball-meme search with empathy, not links

Searching Google for a half-remembered phrase from a 2010s basketball meme produced an AI-generated answer that assumed heartbreak and offered sympathy, with the real links buried far below. It is a funny story, and also a warning about how search now decides what a stranger's question was about.

Checked Searching Google for the phrase "hes never coming over dario" — a 2010s joke about the NBA's Dario Saric — returned an AI Overview that comforted the searcher as if a romantic rejection were the question.

Nish I laughed, then I checked my own product's search results and felt something colder. If a search engine can be talked into a wrong assumption by a half-typed phrase, I should never assume my customers found the page I wanted them to find.

But It is one person's screenshot of one query on one day, and Google ranks these AI answers inconsistently — the behavior is real but the prevalence is unmeasured.
