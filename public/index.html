Anthropic says Sonnet 5.5 runs 30% faster and finishes tasks for less

Anthropic shipped the second model in its 5.5 family, built for everyday work like fixing bugs and drafting documents. The company says it answers more than 30% faster than the version it replaces and does the same work using fewer tokens, the chunks of text these models bill you for. The price per token did not change.

Checked Anthropic says Sonnet 5.5 scores 70.6% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 against 10.3% for Sonnet 5, runs 30%+ faster, and still costs $2 per million input tokens.

Nish My agents run on Sonnet, so a cheaper task means a longer loop I can afford to run. I would still re-run my own jobs before I trust the saving.

But The benchmarks and the cost per task are both Anthropic's own measurements. Nothing about the per token rate moved, so a customer pays full price unless the model really does use fewer tokens.

Shopify now lets AI agents complete a purchase at checkout

Shopify turned on WebMCP for checkout, so an AI agent running inside a shopper's browser can read the checkout screen, change an address or delivery option, and place the order once the buyer approves it. It is going out to all eligible Shopify stores, including Shop Pay, while some retailers block agents from buying at all.

Checked Shopify added three agent tools named get_checkout, update_checkout and complete_checkout, and staff product manager Gil Greenberg wrote that the checkout tools give "structured and efficient APIs" for placing an order.

Nish I get most of my traffic from search, and this changes what a store is for. If Shopify lets an assistant finish the checkout, my own page turns into plumbing and the buying moves somewhere I do not control.

But This is an optional path and only for agents that speak WebMCP inside the browser. Amazon and Adidas have gone the other way and block agent purchases.

AMD is buying World Labs, the lab run by Fei-Fei Li, for $8.2 billion in stock

AMD has a definitive agreement to acquire World Labs, the San Francisco lab that builds AI models for understanding and simulating the physical world. The deal is all stock, so AMD keeps its cash, and World Labs keeps doing research afterwards. Li reports to AMD's chief executive as chief scientist.

Checked AMD values the all-stock deal at approximately $8.2 billion, expects it to close by the end of 2026 subject to regulatory approval, and says Fei-Fei Li will report to CEO Lisa Su as chief scientist.

Nish I read this as a chipmaker buying the model makers so it knows what to build next. World Labs makes the software that trains robots, and that is the workload AMD wants to own.

But Nothing has closed yet. The deal needs regulatory approval, and AMD is paying in stock, so the value on the day it signs is not the value on the day it lands.

Demand signalsTechCrunch

Instinct raised $1 billion a month after its valuation was $2.5 billion

Instinct, a personal assistant that lives in text messages and does errands such as booking travel, paying bills and making phone calls, raised $1 billion at a $10 billion valuation. Sequoia, Benchmark and Coatue put the money in. The company launched its invite only service in August 2026 and has no mobile app.

Checked Instinct's Series C values it at $10 billion, up from the $2.5 billion valuation it announced about a month earlier, and Sequoia Capital, Benchmark Capital and Coatue invested.

Nish Instinct looks to me like proof that buyers pay for an errand finished, not a question answered. A month apart, those two valuations say how fast the appetite for that is growing.

But Instinct has shared no user numbers and has no app, so the price is a bet on the category rather than proof of a business. Meta's Muse does much of the same job and is already built into Facebook and Instagram.

Demand signalsTechCrunch

Outmarket raised $34.5M to automate insurance paperwork

Outmarket sells software that reads and fills in the forms an insurance broker deals with all day, so the office can spend its time with clients instead of paperwork. The company says 300 agencies now use it. Its founder says 95% of insurance is still sold through human agents and that the process is very manual.

Checked Outmarket's Series B was $34.5 million led by SignalFire at a $335 million valuation, four months after a $17 million Series A, and the company says 300 agencies are customers including a quarter of the top 100.

Nish I keep an eye on Outmarket because insurance paperwork is a chore every agency must do. It sells to the agency rather than replacing it, which looks like the easy way into an industry nobody wants to touch.

But The customer count is the company's own number, and rivals like Fulcrum AI and FurtherAI are chasing the same brokers.

Nvidia built a watchdog chip to keep AI agents in their lane

Nvidia announced a platform that puts a second, independent guard beside every AI agent. OpenShell, its open source software, decides what an agent is allowed to reach. Sentry, a reference design, runs on a chip of its own and watches for escape attempts. The company lists Anthropic, Microsoft, Salesforce, Scale AI and SpaceXAI among the partners, and OpenAI is absent from the list.

Checked Nvidia says Sentry runs on BlueField-4 data processing units and can quarantine an agent in milliseconds, and its partner list includes Anthropic, CrowdStrike, Hugging Face, Microsoft, Salesforce and SpaceXAI.

Nish I want this for the agents that touch my accounts, and I also want to know who holds the off switch. A vendor selling the guard for the agents its own chips power, to the labs and companies that buy those chips, is a business model worth watching.

But This is a launch announcement rather than a proven fix. Commenters on Hacker News, 198 of them, argued that no sandbox holds an agent that needs wide access to be useful at all.

Kids turned quiet Spotify comment threads into a group chat

Comments piled up under a podcast episode almost nobody listens to, so a producer at NPR's Wild Card read them as bots and deleted them. A younger colleague looked again and recognised children, who were using a public Spotify playlist named New Chat to find each other before posting under a podcast. One of them came back to complain that a comment had been deleted.

Checked Episode 897 of This American Life, aired September 18, 2026, traced the comments to middle schoolers who found each other through a public Spotify playlist called New Chat.

Nish This one stayed with me all day. A producer looked at the Spotify comments, saw a bot problem, and missed the children. Every product I make has a quiet corner, and I do not know who is already living in it.

But It is one week of comments under one podcast, and the children were never counted. The behavior is real, but its scale is a guess.
