AISpaceXAI

Grok 4.6 is priced for long-running agent work

Grok 4.6 is aimed at work that takes many steps, such as researching a topic, changing a codebase, or turning an idea into a working application. The practical shift is not just a smarter chat answer. It is a model being sold around staying with the job.

Checked SpaceXAI lists pricing of $2 per million input tokens and $6 per million output tokens, with a 2x usage offer in Grok Build and Cursor for the first week.

Nish I see the important move as pricing the model around sustained work rather than one-off questions. If the model can keep checking and refining its own output, that is much closer to a junior operator than a chatbot.

But The benchmark numbers and self-testing claims come from the model maker, so the real test is whether long tasks finish reliably in ordinary projects.

DeepSeek V4 Pro makes cheap agent work more practical

DeepSeek's new V4 Pro release is not positioned as a curiosity. It is a large model with a million-token context window, live provider metrics, and an API price low enough to make repeated agent runs less painful.

Checked OpenRouter lists $0.435 per million input tokens, $0.87 per million output tokens, a 1M context window, and 44 tokens per second median throughput for the provider.

Nish I think this matters more than another leaderboard fight. When a capable model becomes cheap enough to run repeatedly, founders can afford agents that search, retry, and verify instead of stopping after one expensive attempt.

But The release is only a day old and OpenRouter's latency and pricing data are live provider measurements, not a guarantee for every workload.

Tailscale found a 16-year-old SQLite bug after 19 corruptions

Tailscale published the postmortem for repeated control-plane database corruption. The lesson is uncomfortable for any small team: a familiar, dependable technology can still fail when a company pushes it outside the path most users take.

Checked Tailscale says it saw 19 separate database-corruption incidents over six months before fixing the underlying SQLite WAL-reset bug.

Nish I would rather read this SQLite postmortem than another launch post. The useful product lesson is that reliability is not a checkbox you buy with a popular database. You earn it by testing the exact way your business uses the boring parts.

But The affected databases held configuration metadata rather than private keys or network traffic, so the incident was serious but limited in scope.

Lovable raises $400M as app building becomes a business channel

Lovable raised a major round while pushing beyond prototypes into software that runs businesses. Its story is a demand signal for tools that let domain experts build and operate narrow internal products without waiting for a full engineering team.

Checked Lovable says users have created more than 60 million projects, its apps receive over 900 million visits each month, and the round values the company at $13.3 billion.

Nish I do not see the valuation as the whole story. I see a monetization loop: people build a small tool, get real users, and then use the same platform to run more of the business around it. That is a stronger product wedge than a toy app generator.

But These adoption and customer outcomes are company-reported, and the funding announcement is not independent proof that every project becomes a durable business.
