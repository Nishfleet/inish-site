AIMeta AI Research

Muse Glimmer puts a 30B agent on a consumer GPU

Meta released Muse Glimmer as an open-weight model designed for always-on agents that run locally. The practical change is that a capable agent can work on a Mac or PC without sending every task to the cloud.

Checked Meta says its 4-bit version fits under 20 GB and can run inside a 24 GB or 32 GB memory envelope.

Nish I see a real product opening in Muse Glimmer: private local agents that can work on company files even when the internet is unavailable.

But Meta's performance claims are its own benchmarks, and the model still needs integrations such as MLX or llama.cpp for a smooth setup.

AICactus Compute

Needle 2 makes device AI a 14 MB product component

Cactus published Needle 2, a tiny open model for turning speech or text into device actions and structured data. It is aimed at cheap phones, wearables, smart-home products, and small robots rather than general chat.

Checked The published build is 14 MB, uses 28 MB of session RAM, and reports more than 500 tokens per second on a Raspberry Pi 5.

Nish I like Needle's focus: a small device model that reliably turns a request into an action is more useful than a giant chatbot that talks well.

But Needle is specialized; its own benchmark shows weaker results on broad multi-call tasks outside its device-action training.

ToolsTechCrunch

Claude Code will default to fewer permission prompts

Anthropic plans to make Claude Code's auto mode the default for paid teams. The tool will continue on its own unless an action looks irreversible, destructive, or outside the working environment.

Checked Anthropic reported that 1,053 paid testers saw auto mode catch 89% of harmful actions, compared with 13.6% for manual review; users approved 97% of permission prompts.

Nish This is the direction I want from Claude Code auto mode: fewer interruptions, but a hard boundary around destructive actions instead of blind autonomy.

But The safety comparison comes from Anthropic's own test, and the default is scheduled to start on August 14 rather than being live for everyone today.

Product ideasOpenAI

OpenAI prices heavier Business seats at $125

OpenAI is adding Premium seats to ChatGPT Business so a team can give heavy users more capacity without upgrading everyone. Standard seats stay cheaper, while Premium is designed for people doing larger projects with Codex and company data.

Checked Premium costs $125 per user each month, compared with $25 for Standard, and includes five times more usage with no five-hour limit.

Nish I would use the Premium seat idea of seat-level pricing: businesses can keep casual users cheap while charging more for the few people who turn AI into daily infrastructure.

But This is a vendor announcement, and the temporary $100 credit offer ends August 20 for eligible early customers.

Demand signalsFortune

Corma raises $60M to build an AI cyber-defense workforce

Corma emerged from stealth with a large seed round for AI models built specifically to defend corporate networks. The bet is that general-purpose AI is making attackers faster, so defense needs its own specialized AI workforce.

Checked Corma announced $60 million in seed funding led by Sequoia Capital, with Khosla Ventures and Coatue also participating.

Nish I read Corma's funding as a demand signal, not just a headline: security teams are becoming an early buyer for specialized agents that can work at machine speed.

But The round and product claims are newly announced, and reported early-deployment improvements have not been independently confirmed.

WildcardWorld Train Map

World Train Map redraws 1,093 routes from better data

World Train Map is an interactive atlas of passenger rail routes, with the distance, travel time, speed, operator, rolling stock, and opening year for each line. This week's update replaced rough map guesses with route data from OpenStreetMap relations.

Checked The atlas covers 1,247 routes across 120 countries, and the site says 1,093 lines were redrawn with a median four-metre distance from OpenStreetMap.

Nish I take World Train Map as a useful lesson: a small product becomes trustworthy when it publishes the corrections instead of hiding the messy data work behind a polished interface.

But The atlas is not a journey planner and does not sell tickets; several hundred routes still lack complete route-relation data.
