AIInfluencer Magazine

OpenAI cut the price of its cheap model by 80%

OpenAI cut what it charges for its smaller models. These providers bill by the token — roughly a chunk of a word — and the cheapest option now costs a fifth of what it did last month. The company says this comes from efficiency gains, but it lands while business customers are openly pushing back on how much AI costs them.

Checked From 30 July 2026 Luna costs $0.20 per million input tokens and $1.20 per million output, down from $1.00 and $6.00. Mid-tier Terra fell 20%; the top model was left alone.

Nish I run my agents on exactly this tier, so Luna at a fifth of the old price changes what I can afford to leave working overnight.

But This is a trade-press write-up and I could not find OpenAI's own announcement, so treat the figures as reported rather than confirmed.

AIMartin Alderson

Why AI prices are falling, and who was making the money

The big labs make most of their profit not from building models but from renting them out afterwards, where the markup is very large. Cheaper open rivals have now got close enough on quality that the markup is getting hard to justify. That is the pressure behind the cuts in the story above.

Checked He puts frontier lab pricing near $25 per million tokens on roughly a 90% gross margin, while the open GLM5.2 runs about $4.40 — under 20% of Opus's retail price.

Nish If the margin really is 90%, every price cut I am offered is a fight for my business rather than a favour, and I should keep moving.

But These are one analyst's estimates worked out from public price lists, not audited accounts. Nobody outside the labs knows the true cost of serving a request.

ToolsMartin Alderson

Picking your AI on speed, not brains

The top models are now good enough at ordinary work, so the one that answers fastest is often the better buy. He also shows why a much faster model does not make the whole job much faster: waiting on the model is only part of the time, and the rest does not speed up with it.

Checked A task at 50 tokens a second takes 65 seconds start to finish; at 250 it still takes 33. Making the model five times quicker made the actual job only twice as fast.

Nish I pick on brains every time, and this says I am paying for cleverness I never actually use on most of my day's tasks.

But He is timing his own working day rather than running a formal test, and a quick answer that is wrong costs more than a slow one that is right.

Product ideasKorix

How AI companies charge is changing fast

Charging a flat monthly fee per user is dying out for AI products, because two customers paying the same can cost you wildly different amounts to serve. What is replacing it: a base subscription plus a bill for going over an allowance, the way a phone plan works.

Checked Bessemer, tracking 200+ AI vendors, puts hybrid pricing at 41% in 2026 against 27% in 2025, while per-seat pricing fell from 21% to 15% of SaaS in a year.

Nish Everything I sell is priced per user, and a fall from 21% to 15% in a single year says I am pricing on last year's playbook.

But This summarises Bessemer's report rather than being the report, and counting vendors is not counting revenue — plenty of money still sits in per-seat deals.

Demand signalsSlopfix

Businesses will pay $10,000 to delete AI's code

A three-person team sells exactly one thing: a week of tearing out the bloat that AI coding tools leave behind. It is a real service at a real price, and it only exists because so many companies now own software nobody on the team understands.

Checked The offer is "One week. Three senior engineers. $10,000", paid in proportion to the result, with one example cutting a codebase from 100,000 lines to 35,000.

Nish This is the sharpest demand signal I have seen all week: three engineers making a living cleaning up after the tools I use every single day.

But They publish no client count and no revenue, so it shows people will pay the asking price, not that the market behind it is big.

AI budgets are still growing, fast

Company spending on AI is not slowing down. The forecast covers the whole stack — the machines, the software, the services, and the newer tools that do work on their own. For anyone selling to businesses, the budget is sitting there.

Checked Gartner forecasts $2.59 trillion of worldwide AI spending in 2026, up 47% year over year, in a press release dated 19 May 2026.

Nish A 47% rise in spending tells me money is not what is stopping my buyers, so when something of mine does not sell, that one is on me.

But A forecast is not a receipt, and this total is mostly hardware and consulting — very different from budget available for a small software product.

A benchmark where everyone passes and nobody scores

Someone asks fourteen AI models, three times a month, to draw a frog with a Habsburg jaw. The fun is not really the drawings. It is watching which models cannot resist editorialising about the frog's chin instead of simply drawing what was asked.

Checked All 42 attempts across 14 models produced a valid drawing in August 2026, and the site publishes no score of any kind.

Nish I read leaderboards as though they were a score, and 42 out of 42 is a fine reminder that a test everyone passes is measuring nothing.

But Three attempts per model per month, judged purely on taste, so it is a curiosity rather than evidence about any of the models in it.
